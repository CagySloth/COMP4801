## Abstract

Haplotype phasing is an important part of genomic analysis as it puts heterozygous variants on the chromosome they came from. Long-read sequencing technologies (e.g., Oxford Nanopore Technologies, ONT) yield reads that can encompass numerous variants and enhance phasing contiguity. Practically, however, phasing performance depends on the effectiveness of the upstream alignment and variant calling, as well as the long-read characteristics such as uneven coverage, repetitive regions, and structured error distribution patterns. To fully understand how WhatsHap, a commonly used phasing tool for long reads phasing, operate in real-world situations, a controlled and repeatable benchmarking environment is required.

This project aims to create a reusable benchmarking pipeline, capable of synthesizing diploid reference genome, phased ground-truth variants and haplotypes, generate reads through ONT-like sequencing simulation, with configurable realism stressors, aligns reads and calls variant through external toolsets, phases simulated datasets through a vendored WhatsHap core, and offer evaluation results of phased outputs against truth haplotypes. To separate attribution of phaser-limited and caller-limited behavior, both oracle and called variant sets are available in the framework. Unit tests and end-to-end test runs are performed to ensure that the implementation could work effectively and as expected. Ultimately, the framework is used for ablations and parameter sweeps to detect failure modes and discover how to optimize WhatsHap for practical use and long-read phasing workflows.
