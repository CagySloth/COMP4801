## 12. Conclusion

This project investigated WhatsHap wMEC-based long-read phasing performanace by building a synthetic benchmarking framework designed to control and balance realism, attribution breakdown, and experimental flexibility. The main objective of this study was not only an evaluation on WhatsHap's phasing performance, but also to analyze sources of performance loss in a practical end-to-end scenario and to explore directions for end-to-end optimization potential.

The project offers three main contributions in the optimization research for WhatsHap. First, a highly configurable benchmarking pipeline, with approximated realism knobs, and covers both oracle and called framework is constructed and tested. This oracle-vs-called design allows experiments to separate phaser-limited behavior from end-to-end behavior, making attribution of losses to variant recovery, phasing completeness, or phasing correctness possible. The multiple implemented realism knobs also support approximation of realistic data characteristics, such as duplication, coverage dropout, correlated error bursts, variable read-length, and truth indels with SNP-only evaluation as a safeguard. With these realism knobs, the benchmarking pipeline can not only conduct experiments on phasing performance, but also investigate various impact on WhatsHap's phasing algorithms from different realism stressors.

Second, experiment results indicated that, the configuration of the phasing algorithm itself has less impact to end-to-end phasing performance when compared to the quality and overlap of informative heterozygous variant sites coming from upstream processing. In the oracle framework, WhatsHap's phasing results remained highly accurate when correct variant site information is available. On the other hand, in the called framework, missing shared heterozygous sites and reduced connectivity have led to larger degradation in phasing accuracy. Among all the isolated realism knobs tested in the pipeline, the strongest standalong stressor was coverage dropout, affecting phasing performance through fragmentation and reduction in variant recovery. The compounded effect between duplication and dropout revealed that, in practical settings, effect on phasing difficulty from realism stressors are often compounded instead of purely additive, with mixed caller-side and phaser-side weaknesses becoming most obvious under combined stress.

Third, although limited, beneficial optimization headroom was shown from the optimization analysis. In this study, the most significant optimization outcome resulted from adjustment in caller-side base-quality threshold. Experiments showed that call recall, shared heterozygous overlap, and end-to-end phased recall, all improved by reducing `call_min_baseq` to a moderate value, without showing substantial compromization in precision. On the other hand, while adjustments on `min_baseq` on the phasing side did not yield much optimization potential, it is shown that higher `min_baseq` values, such as 20, could be consistently harmful due to overly strict filtering, and moderate values, such as 10, stayed on a stable performance plateau. There is also a tradeoff between runtime and accuracy shown from adjusting retained coverage, with `max_coverage` = 8-10, phasing performance is preserved without too much computation stress. Therefore, with this benchmarking pipeline, the best optimized general configuration is with `call_min_baseq` = 10, `max_coverage` = 8, `min_baseq` = 10, `call_min_mapq` = 20, and `min_mapq` = 20. An alternative configuration with `max_coverage` = 6 showed almost identical phasing performance, with a potentially higher computational efficiency, which could be useful when efficiency is prioritized.

When combined, a pipeline-level view of WhatsHap optimization was supported by experiment results. Although WhatsHap demonstrated exceptional robustness when sufficient variant site information is available, end-to-end phasing performance is influenced by upstream processes significantly. Therefore, practical optimization gains are most likely coming from preserving information throughout the pipeline, and from moderate call-side and phaser-side parameter adjustments combined.

Several future directions follow naturally from this study, including validation research on the recommended parameter configurations on more realistic datasets, upstream variant recovery study, more in depth algorithmic analysis on WhatsHap's wMEC solver, and region-aware optimization approaches under compounded stress. In summary, this project offers an evaluation platform with reproducibility, and a set of optimized parameter configurations validated by experiments for more effective WhatsHap-based long-read phasing.
