## 11. Discussion

### 11.1 Discussion of main findings

This study aimed to understand WhatsHap performance under realistic long-read conditions, identify where performance is lost in an end-to-end pipeline, and determine whether practical optimization opportunities exist. The results show that these goals were achieved at three levels: attribution, stress characterization, and optimization.

First, the baseline depth study established a clear attribution pattern. In the oracle regime, WhatsHap remained highly accurate once correct variant sites were available. In the called regime, however, a large share of end-to-end performance loss arose from reduced overlap of correctly called heterozygous variants rather than from the phasing algorithm itself. This reveals a significant implication for optimization, that is, in realistic settings, the primary bottleneck may not be the phasing algorithm itself. End-to-end phasing recall improvements could rely as much on variant recovery as on the effectiveness of phasing.

Second, from the realism stressor studies, it is observed that, the phasing pipeline is affected differently by different disruptions from realism knobs. The most significant standalone stressor appeared to be coverage dropout, primarily reducing phasing completeness by fragmenting phase blocks, and also reducing variant recovery effectiveness at higher severity. Duplicated regions were comparatively mild under the tested parameterization, with their main effect appearing only at the strongest setting and mostly in the called regime. Correlated error bursts produced a weaker and more variable signal than expected, while the read-length model showed a mixed trade-off: better continuity and overlap, but no substantial end-to-end gain because called-regime correctness became slightly noisier. The indel study mainly served as a methodology-validation result, showing that SNP-only safeguards preserve interpretable evaluation when truth indels are present.

Third, the interaction study showed that the most informative failure modes do not necessarily come from isolated knobs alone. Duplication combined with dropout produced a substantially worse end-to-end condition than either stressor individually, especially in the called regime. This indicates that realistic phasing difficulty can arise from compounded weaknesses: ambiguous mapping worsens low-coverage conditions by simultaneously reducing callable overlap and weakening phasing completeness. This result was particularly useful because it connected the stress studies to the optimization studies: the most important practical weaknesses are mixed, rather than purely phaser-limited.

Finally, from the optimization study, it is showed that, although there are potential for optimization, but the benefit is limited. After experimenting with different configurations, caller-side base-quality tuning offered the most significant useful improvement. By lowering `call_min_baseq` from an overly strict filtering threshold, variant recovery, shared heterozygous overlap, and end-to-end phased recall all improved, without obvious drawback on precision observed. On the other hand, the most noticeable insight on the phasing side, was also to avoid overly strict filtering. At `min_baseq` = 20, filtering proved to be consistently detrimental, yet at moderate value such as 10, phasing performance remained stable. Additionally, a practical tradeoff between runtime and phasing performance is demonstrated in adjusting retained coverage, `max_coverage`. At `max_coverage` = 8-10, phasing performance is preserved without too much unnecessary computation.
In contrast, both caller-side and phasing-side mapping-quality thresholds provided little useful headroom.

The SNP-density scaling study yielded an additional finding that was relevant to WhatsHap's phasing algorithm. It is observed that downstream solve stage became more expensive as variant density increased, however, the optimized configuration substantially outscaled the default configurations, suggesting that not only is the final phasing output performance improved, the difficulty of the residual wMEC problem for the solver is also altered by the optimized parameter configuration. Even thought the project-specific readset construction adaptor dominated the runtime of the phasing pipeline, this finding still uphold the belief that both phasing quality and solver computation difficulty thorugh practical parameter tuning. 

In summary, the results show that, WhatsHap should not be evaluated and studied on a standalone basis, instead, WhatsHap should be studied as one of the components of a larger pipeline, that stretches from genome sequencing all the way to variant calling and finally phasing. When given correct variant information, WhatsHap is shown to be robust and accurate, however, this also indicated that WhatsHap's end-to-end phasing performance is significantly reliant on upstream variant recovery, filtering, and connectivity disruptions. Thus, optimization opportunities for WhatsHap exist, but instead of being within WhatsHap's phasing algorithms, they are mostly at the end-to-end pipeline level.

### 11.2 Practical implications for WhatsHap-based phasing

From a practical standpoint for WhatsHap-based long-read phasing workflows, several specific recommendations were suggested by the experiment results.

First, interpretation of end-to-end phasing performance shoud not be solely based on phasing metrics. Across all the tested experiment suites, the primary degradation in phasing performance happened before or concurrent with phasing, due to reduced overlap of accurately called heterozygous variants, particularly at moderate depth or under compounded stress. Thus, both variant recovery and phasing quality should be evaluated simultaneously, as strong oracle results does not translated to strong end-to-end phasing performance when the called VCFs are not able to preserve sufficient heterozygous site information.

Second, conservative filtering may be counterproductive in optimizing phasing quality. On the caller side, too much true variant information could be removed from overly strict base-quality filtering, thus substantially reducing downstream phased recall rates. On the phasing side, connectivity and fragmantation could be worsened from overly aggressive thresholds in base-quality filtering. In the current pipeline, conservative configurations are almost always outperformed by more moderate settings. Thus, variant information should be preserved when there is not a good reason to discard it, in both the caller and phaser.

Third, practical parameter adjustment should include decisions from both the caller and phasing side. In this study, the best-performing configuration was achieved by optimizing both the caller and phaser simultaneously, instead of optimizing either the caller or the phaser in isolation. Moreover, the optimized parameter configuration demonstrated good generalizing capability across baseline, dropout, interactions, and harder scenarios, in the frontier and transferability research. Thus, this study supports the recommendation of a set of tuned parameter configuration over singular parameter adjustment.

Fourth, not every intuitive optimization path is worth exploring. From the optimization experiments, it is observed that, increasing `max_coverage`, caller-side and phasing-side mapping quality thresholds were not effective in optimizing phasing performance. Such negative findings are helpful in narrowing the search space for optimization opportunity, and helps indicate areas where additional tuning effort are unlikely to yield benefits.

Finally, several significant remaining optimization options exist in the upstream of the phasing solver. It is found that, enhancements in variant recovery are likely to be more impactful than further refinement of phasing thresholds, as phasing performance was primarily driven by missing shared heterozygous site. Although this helps clarify where practical optimization are likely to come from in the future, it does not make WhatsHap tuning any less valuable.

### 11.3 Limitations of the present study

Several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting the results.

First, a synthetic data generation pipeline is used for this project, instead of real biological truth sets, to enable total control over realism stressors, such as dropout, duplications and error bursts, allow oracle-vs-called comparison for attribution, and avoid overfitting to a given dataset. However, synthetic truth still simplifies some aspects of real sequencing data and genome structure. The absolute metric values reported here should therefore be interpreted as controlled experimental measurements rather than direct predictions of production performance on all real datasets.

Second, some realism knobs produced weaker or noisier signals than expected. In particular, correlated error bursts did not show a strong monotonic degradation trend under the tested parameterization, and duplication alone was milder than expected except at the strongest setting. This does not invalidate the experiments, but it means that some conclusions are better interpreted as ruling out large effects under the current settings rather than as definitive evidence that a stressor is unimportant in general.

Third, the phasing pipeline used a custom adaptor layer to construct the `ReadSet` consumed by the vendored WhatsHap core. This adaptor was necessary for the project’s experimental interface, including support for oracle/called benchmarking, unified JSON provenance, and controlled SNP-only evaluation. Runtime profiling showed that the dominant runtime cost in the research pipeline lies in this adaptor rather than in the solver itself. For this reason, the runtime results should be interpreted carefully: they are informative about the project’s benchmarking infrastructure, but they do not necessarily represent the standard production-facing WhatsHap front-end directly.

Fourth, while some practical recommendations were identified through the optimization research sweeps, they are not representative of the whole parameter space. Therefore, instead of being mathematically proven optima, recommendation settings from this study should be regarded as credible experimentally validated options for optimization. Although it is shown that the chosen threshold configuration is effective locally from the local search, more extensive or adaptive parameter exploration could still be rewarding in the future.

Fifth, to ensure control within the project scope and time constraints, the transferability and scaling studies were conducted in a brief format intentionally. Although these studies could be sufficient to validate the main conclusions regarding reliability and scalability of the solver, larger scale benchmarking with practical biological dataset should still be performed before adopting adjustments in practical scenarios.

### 11.4 Future work

This research leads to several possible extensions in the future.

One initial direction is to evaluate the proposed optimized parameter configuration on larger and more realistic genome datasets, such as real long-read data and more complex synthetic datasets. Such research would be beneficial in the evaluation of transferability of the recommended configurations beyond the controlled simulation pipeline in this study.

A second direction is to study upstream variant recovery more directly. Since many end-to-end losses were shown to be caller-limited, a natural extension would be to compare different variant callers, different calling models, or more adaptive caller-side filtering strategies. The present work suggests that this may be a more promising route to further gains than continued refinement of phasing thresholds alone.

A third direction is to deepen the algorithm analysis of the residual phasing problem in the solver. The SNP-density scaling study already suggests that tuning can change solver difficulty, but a richer study could investigate how variant density, read connectivity, and overlap structure jointly affect the residual wMEC problem instance presented to the DP solver.

A fourth direction is to compare the project-specific adaptor against the standard WhatsHap front-end more directly. This would help separate properties of the benchmarking interface from properties of the production-facing workflow. This comparison is particularly important for runtime interpretation and optimization.

A fifth direction is to explore optimization strategies that is region-aware, such as adaptive filtering and region-aware read selection, for complex regions under compounded realism stress. From the interaction study between duplication and dropout, it is observed that complex regions and low-coverage gaps may require different handling strategies, compared with simpler regions. Thus, future research could explore adaptive filtering, and region-aware strategies that are able to adapt to local information quality, instead of pursuing globally fixed configurations and strategies.

### 11.5 Conclusion

In summary, a pipeline-level optimization of WhatsHap is supported by this study. Experiments from this study were able to show that, upstream variant recovery, connectivity, and compounded realistic stress, are crucial in end-to-end phased recall rate. On the other hand, moderate caller-side and phasing-side filtering, instead of isolated parameter adjustments, yields the best improvements in end-to-end phasing performance. These findings leads to the final conclusions summarized in Section 12.
